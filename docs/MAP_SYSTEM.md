# Map system and geographic audit

## Executable map

`index.html` has one inline SVG (`viewBox="0 0 890 1235"`) after the worksheet `<img>`. Both inherit `.mapArea`; SVG scales to container width with `height:auto`, mobile rearranges the panel but does not change SVG coordinates. The background is a sea rectangle; land is painted above water zones. Geometry is stored as SVG paths in `ITEMS[].shape`, `CONTEXT_LAND` (19 surrounding countries), and composite `italy`. Five quiz-country silhouettes are `area-i..m`; islands are separate paths. Map positions are **SVG pixels**, while the embedded `x/y/r` fields are older percentage-like metadata, not geographic coordinates. The paths were apparently projected into SVG using approximately `x=(longitude-4.1)*55`, `y=(49.75-latitude)*77` during earlier production, but that transform and upstream data source are **not encoded/provenanced in the runtime**. It is an audit inference, not a guaranteed transformation contract.

Layers from bottom to top: `seaLayer` (Mediterranean/sea polygons), `landLayer` (context land, five quiz countries, Italy, islands), `areaLayer` (mountains/regions), `riverLayer` (Po/Tiber strokes), `labels` (static context), `pointLayer` (volcano triangles, microstate dots, city dots and transparent microstate hit circles), `nameLayer` (Learn-only labels/leaders). No separately stored GIS file. `addShape` puts ID and keyboard listener on each path; point targets are circles. Most cities have radius 6.5 SVG units, Rome 6.3; San Marino has a 4.5-unit visible mark and transparent hit circle; Vatican has a 2.7-unit visible mark and Rome/Vatican share a separate transparent 15-unit circle with a 22-unit invisible stroke. Hover/focus/tap exposes a small local selector offering two independently graded targets. The small schematic in the lens is an educational selector, not a magnified cadastral map. Other point/river touch tolerances are still tied to the visual shapes. River hit stroke is 8 units. SVG scaling reduces physical tap sizes on phones.

`NAME_ANCHORS` holds area/sea/river learning labels; most city labels default to a right/up offset, with custom left-side anchors for Bari, Bologna, Venetië and Triëst. Labels have white stroke and ignore pointer events. Quiz hides learning labels. Five quiz-country shapes are SVG paths; sea and mountain/regional hit polygons were authored didactically; rivers are smooth manual SVG paths, not audited GIS linework. Volcanoes use small triangular paths and `▲` glyph. Italy's coastline is coarse near lagoons and small coastal cities.

**Printable map:** `scripts/generate-print.mjs` reads the same embedded paths, worksheet JPEG, item codes and geographic anchors to generate `print.html`. Its clean map uses the current SVG geometry; the alternate original school photo uses the same 890 × 1235 viewBox. For printable city codes, the generator chooses an **editorial** offset that clears every visible city dot in the group, places a short leader line to the unchanged dot, and records the positions in `DATA.printLabels`. The A4 PDF booklet generated by `scripts/generate-pdf.py` uses exactly those positions and the photographed worksheet; both paper tests share them. The original dots/learning coordinates do not move. The PDF generator writes `output/pdf/print-source.sha256`; `npm run build` refuses a stale PDF. Neither print output is a second geographic authority. The photo in the PDF may consume more ink than the clean HTML map.

## Worksheet orientation

The school worksheet is embedded as a **890 × 1235** JPEG photo and toggled over an **890 × 1235** SVG with `position:absolute`, `object-fit:fill` and opacity. An image-pixel audit of red worksheet dots against runtime `ITEMS[].px/py` found close correspondence across the frame: Bologna approximately (402,406) versus (401,406), Venetië (457,336) versus (453,333), Triëst (538,322) versus (535,313), Bari (717,663) versus (708,669). These are photographed-dot centroid estimates, not geodetic controls. Both layers have the same ratio, so `object-fit:fill` does **not** introduce additional aspect-ratio stretch at the normal viewBox ratio. The photographed page frame has perspective/skew and its coastlines are simplified differently; global rotation or a guessed homography would move already-close worksheet dots away from interactive targets. Chosen approach: preserve both orientations and their 1:1 overlay, keep the photographed worksheet readable, and defer any photo warp until independent coastline/control-point registration proves a net accuracy gain. Do not rotate the learning SVG to match camera angle. There is no independently validated geospatial registration yet.

## Long-term map contract

Use one licensed/versioned geospatial source and explicit CRS/projection/viewBox transform. Store stable geographic coordinates for points and feature geometry for land, seas and rivers; generate SVG drawing from it. Keep `(1)` source geographic geometry, `(2)` rendered visual shape, `(3)` hit shape/tolerance and `(4)` editorial label anchor separate. Tiny states get a local two-target lens. Validate city/microstate coordinates against intended country, labels against polygons or declared leader lines, routes against source lines, and representative render screenshots at multiple sizes. This is the next implementation package, not a silent transformation performed by this documentation task.

## Route screen and Rome selector (2026-09-28)

The route overview is a sibling of the map and practice panel in `.layout`; CSS hides the map only in `routeHomeLayout`. Active lessons switch to `routeLessonLayout`, retain the same 890 × 1235 SVG, and expose a sticky mobile question dock. No geographic coordinates are modified by the route UI. The Rome/Vatican inset draws a schematic Rome city oval with Vatican inside it, identifies it as **not to scale**, and offers two separately graded buttons. The oval illustrates administrative containment and does not purport to be city boundary geometry. Main-map point anchors remain subject to external GIS verification.
